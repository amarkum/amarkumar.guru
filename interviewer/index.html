Know your interviewer

Prepare for the person, not just the problem

Your interviewer is a senior engineer (SDE III) at Amazon. This page covers what that usually means for the round, the kind of questions to expect, and what's worth a quick look at on her public profile before the call.

What an SDE III looks for

  • Ownership of the whole design. Don't stop at the happy path. Talk about what happens when things fail, how you'd know, and how you'd fix it.
  • Depth under push-back. She'll keep asking “why?” until you reach the edge of what you know. That's expected. Stay calm and think out loud.
  • Clean, extensible code. Clear names, small pieces, and a design where adding the next feature doesn't mean rewriting everything.
  • Trade-offs stated clearly. When you choose something, say what you're giving up, like simple vs scalable or fast vs always correct.
  • Whether they'd want you on their team. Think of the hour as working through a design with a future teammate.

What she is likely to ask

Expect a hands-on design round with plenty of follow-up questions, plus one or two “tell me about a time” questions. These are the most likely topics. Don't mention in the interview that you looked her up.

Most likely design prompts

  1. Payment / wallet / refund: charge a customer, never double-charge, refund safely. Expect idempotency keys, PENDING → SUCCESS/FAILED, “bank timed out — what now?”, and a reconciliation job.
  2. Ledger or billing: double-entry ledger, append-only corrections, invoicing or seller payouts. Correctness before latency.
  3. Custom problem from her team: syncing money or status across services, retry storms, or a small LLD she lives with daily. Clarify requirements carefully; she knows every edge case.
  4. Classic LLD with an “add this” twist: rate limiter, notification router, parking lot + peak pricing, file search + filters, shipping cost calculator. Extend via Strategy/Factory — don't rewrite.
  5. AWS-shaped follow-ups: when would you pick DynamoDB vs SQL? SQS vs SNS? How do you make a Lambda (or worker) idempotent? What goes in S3 vs the DB?

Follow-ups she will almost certainly fire

  • “Two requests hit at once — same payment / last inventory unit. What happens?”
  • “The client retries. How do you not charge twice?”
  • “This dependency is down. What's the customer experience, and how do you recover?”
  • “How do you monitor this? What alarms? How do you roll back a bad deploy?”
  • “How would you change this if volume grew 10×?”

Likely Leadership Principles questions

  • Ownership: “A problem outside your scope that you still owned end to end.”
  • Dive Deep: “A hard production bug — how did you find root cause?”
  • Customer Obsession: “A time you changed the design because of customer impact.”
  • Deliver Results / Bias for Action: “Tight deadline — what did you cut and why?”
  • Earn Trust / Have Backbone: “You disagreed with a senior engineer. What happened?”
  • Learn and Be Curious / Mentorship: “A time you leveled someone up, or learned a new domain fast.”

How to play the hour

  • Talk to her like a teammate you're reviewing a design with. It's fine to ask, “Is this close to what your team works on?”
  • With money, say early that getting it right matters more than being fast, and bring up retries and duplicate payments before she asks.
  • If you mention an AWS service, say why, for example “SQS, because the work can be retried safely”.
  • Keep your code tidy and your names clear. Senior engineers notice that.
  • In your stories, say “I”, not “we”, and finish with a real result, ideally a number.

Two-minute check on her public profile

Only look at work details. Use them to guess what kind of problem might come up, and never bring the profile up during the call.

  • Level
    SDE III at Amazon She'll expect you to think about failures, monitoring and trade-offs, not just draw boxes.
  • Team
    FinTech → payments, billing, invoicing, payouts, tax, ledgers. Practice one end to end.
  • Tech
    AWS-heavy → be ready on DynamoDB, SQS, SNS, Lambda, S3, and when you'd pick each.
  • Tenure
    Years at Amazon → deeper LP scoring. Polish 4–5 STAR stories the night before.
  • Background
    May mean a custom integration or reliability question, not a textbook “Design Twitter.”

Do

  • Ask early: “Is this close to something your team works on?”
  • Connect choices to their customer (sellers, buyers, finance teams)
  • Thank them for hints and use them
  • Ask one real question about her team at the end

Don't

  • Mention that you looked her up or quote her profile
  • Try to flatter or name-drop
  • Send connection requests or messages before the interview
  • Guess her team out loud; let her tell you

Questions that land well with an SDE III

  • “What's a design decision your team made recently that you'd make differently now?”
  • “How do you handle operational load and on-call when money is involved?”
  • “What separates engineers who grow fast on this team?”
  • “What's the hardest reliability problem the team is solving right now?”
